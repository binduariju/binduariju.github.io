**Authors:** **Riju Bindua**, Thomas Brüstle, Luis Scoccola

This project began during a summer internship at the Université de Sherbrooke, where I had the opportunity to learn about topological data analysis through the language of representation theory.

Persistent homology is exceptionally well understood for filtrations indexed by a line, but much less is known for more general indexing categories. In this work we investigate filtrations indexed by rooted trees, show that the resulting zero-dimensional persistent homology admits a rich algebraic structure, and develop an efficient algorithm for decomposing it into indecomposable pieces.
