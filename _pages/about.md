Hello and welcome! I am a PhD student in Computer Science at McGill University, where I am very fortunate to be co-supervised by [Hamed Hatami](https://www.cs.mcgill.ca/~hatami/) and [Robert Robere](https://www.cs.mcgill.ca/~robere/) in McGill's Complexity Group.

Mathematics has always been my first love. I have always enjoyed exploring its many different branches, and over time I have found myself especially drawn to anything and everything combinatorial.

At the moment, I am working mostly on questions in computational complexity and learning theory. I am still figuring out which directions I would like to pursue, and I am quite happy to keep exploring. I tend to be drawn to problems with a combinatorial flavour, even when they come from very different areas. So far, that curiosity has taken me through online algorithms, geometry, representation theory, and persistent homology, and I am excited to see where it takes me next.

Before moving to Montreal for my PhD, I completed my undergraduate degree at IIT Delhi. During my time there, I was fortunate to learn from wonderful mentors such as Professor [Thomas Brüstle](https://www.usherbrooke.ca/mathematiques/nous-joindre/personnel/corps-professoral/professeurs/thomas-brustle) and Professor [Naveen Garg](https://www.cse.iitd.ac.in/~naveen/), who also kindly supervised my undergraduate thesis.

I enjoy thinking deeply and making space for quiet moments to introspect and philosophize about life. Sometimes those thoughts turn into overthinking; when they do, I usually find my way back through family, friends, or a good YouTube rabbit hole. I also find a lot of peace simply wandering through local parks or heading out to catch a beautiful sunrise or sunset.

I am always happy to talk about mathematics, especially when the conversation begins with a genuine question or an interesting idea. If you would like to chat about complexity theory, combinatorics, or simply exchange mathematical ideas over coffee, please feel free to reach out. And if the conversation happens to end with a walk to watch the sunset somewhere in Montreal, even better.
